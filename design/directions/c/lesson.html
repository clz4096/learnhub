The definition

In general, the share of BBB's outcomes that are also in AAA is P(A∩B)/P(B)P(A \cap B)/P(B)P(A∩B)/P(B). "Learning BBB does not change the chance of AAA" says this share equals P(A)P(A)P(A). Multiply both sides by P(B)P(B)P(B) and you get a cleaner statement, with no division, which is the one we take as the definition.

Why the product form? It treats AAA and BBB symmetrically, and it makes sense even when P(B)=0P(B) = 0P(B)=0, where the share P(A∩B)/P(B)P(A \cap B)/P(B)P(A∩B)/P(B) would mean dividing by zero.

Why is the share P(A∩B)/P(B)P(A \cap B)/P(B)P(A∩B)/P(B)?

With equally likely outcomes, the share is ∣A∩B∣/∣B∣|A \cap B| / |B|∣A∩B∣/∣B∣. Divide top and bottom by the total number of outcomes, 36 for two dice, and it becomes P(A∩B)/P(B)P(A \cap B)/P(B)P(A∩B)/P(B). For the dice: 3/63/63/6 is 3/366/36\frac{3/36}{6/36}6/363/36​. This share is called the conditional probability of AAA given BBB, a lesson of its own.
