# The app's language

Dated 2026-10-08. Status: approved by Albert 2026-10-08. How problem statements, feedback, hints, solutions, and app messages are worded; the rules they serve are in [HOW-A-TOPIC-WORKS.md](HOW-A-TOPIC-WORKS.md).

Decided by Albert, round 1:
- **Problem statements:** examiner style (A). Short, exact, no framing or warmth. "Evaluate ...", "Prove that ...".
- **Not right yet:** a mix of patient tutor and supervisor. Says it isn't right, then one short pointer toward a faster or better route.
- **Hints:** supervisor style (C). Questions, one nudge at a time, never the answer.
- **Solutions:** supervisor style (C). Short steps, ends with the one idea to remember.
- **Maths:** symbols (3/2, not "three halves").
- **Encouragement:** a little.
- **Voice:** neutral. No "you" or "we".

Reference example (fractions, A6 Q1(i)):
- Problem: Evaluate (1 + 1/2)(1 + 1/4)(1 + 1/6)(1 + 1/8) / (1 - 1/2)(1 - 1/4)(1 - 1/6)(1 - 1/8).
- Not right yet: Not quite. Multiplying everything out is the slow route; something here cancels.
- Hint 1: What is 1 + 1/2 as a single fraction? And 1 - 1/2?
- Hint 2: With every bracket as one fraction, which numbers appear both above and below the line?
- Solution: Each bracket becomes one fraction: 3/2 over 1/2, 5/4 over 3/4, and so on. The denominators 2, 4, 6, 8 cancel, then 3, 5, 7 cancel, leaving 9. Simplify first, multiply last.

Decided by Albert, round 2 (proof problems):
- **Hints:** three escalating questions.
- **"A proof needs":** a short list (state the claim in general; letters, not examples; check every condition; state the conclusion).
- **Supervisor comments:** say plainly what is missing (not as questions). Mark first, what is right, then each gap as a plain statement, then what a pass needs.
- **Solutions:** keep the italic one-line lesson at the end.

Reference supervisor comment:
12/20. The algebra is right. Three things stop it being a proof:
1. The argument uses 1/2 + 1/3 only; a proof needs general a/b and c/d.
2. The denominator bd is never shown to be non-zero.
3. The last line is a number, not a statement of what was proved.
Fix these three and it passes.

Decided by Albert, round 3 (lessons and app messages):
- **Lessons:** keep the current form: formal definition, then fuller "In plain words" explanation (version A).
- **App messages:** neutral (no "you"): "Practice passed.", "Sequences: said finished, but its Cambridge problem is still open."
- **Greeting:** keep the name: "Good morning, Albert."
- **Shabbat and yom tov:** plain, no wishes: "Shabbat. Nothing scheduled until Shabbat ends."

Decided by Albert, round 4 (practice feedback):
- **Wrong answer:** version A as now: name the likely mistake, then the rule and how to fix it. ("It looks like you added the tops and added the bottoms. Fractions only add directly when they share a denominator: first rewrite both over a common denominator, then add the numerators.")
- **Right answer:** "Right: 5/12." (repeat the answer).
- **After a miss:** the explanation, then one more try on the same problem, then the worked solution if still wrong.
- Open: A uses "you", which the neutral rule otherwise avoids (see the question to Albert).
- Albert: keep "you" in practice feedback (exception to neutral voice).
