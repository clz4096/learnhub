# How a topic works

Dated 2026-10-08. Status: approved by Albert 2026-10-08 (draft 3). The rules a topic follows from lesson to mastery; the app's wording is in [APP-LANGUAGE.md](APP-LANGUAGE.md).

## The words
- **Topic:** one idea, like Fractions or Direct proof. The book is a list of topics in order.
- **Lesson:** the teaching for a topic, in named sections.
- **Practice:** short problems the app makes up and marks. Unlimited, with feedback.
- **Cambridge problem:** a real problem from a Cambridge source (STEP, Tripos example sheets).
- **Gate:** the Cambridge problems that decide whether you have mastered the topic.
- **Learned:** you finished the lesson and passed its practice. Enough to move on.
- **Mastered:** you solved one of the topic's Cambridge problems on your own.

## Rule 1: a problem only appears once everything it needs has been taught
Every Cambridge problem sits in the earliest topic where all the skills it needs have been taught. A problem that needs fractions and proof writing sits in the proof topic, not in Fractions.

## Rule 2: the order you meet things in
1. Read the lesson.
2. Pass the practice. The topic is **learned**; the next topics open.
3. Try a Cambridge problem. Above it the app says what it tests and what it builds on.
4. Solve one on your own and the topic is **mastered**.

## Rule 3: a miss gets comments and a hint, never the answer
- **Single-answer problems:** "Not right yet," plus a hint. The problem comes back in a few days. A right answer then still counts.
- **Written answers (proofs, explanations):** a supervision marks it out of 20 and says what to fix, without giving the solution. 14 or more passes. Try again when ready.
- **Show me the solution** is always there. Using it means that problem no longer counts; the topic's other Cambridge problems still can.

## Rule 4: the safety net
If a problem ever needs something not yet taught, the supervisor says so. You are pointed to that topic, and the topic you were on is not held against you.

## Rule 5: the final check is a timed mixed paper
Like the Cambridge end-of-year exams, timed papers mix topics. The timed ladder and mixed review are where everything comes together.

## Rule 6: mastery has to last (cold retests)
- Solving one Cambridge problem on your own makes a topic **mastered**, as above.
- About **7 days** later, a **different** Cambridge problem from that topic comes back cold, with no lesson first. Again at about **30 days**.
- Pass, and the topic stays mastered. Miss, and the topic goes back to **needs review**: hints as usual, and it is mastered again when the next one is solved.
- One lucky solve is not enough, and the retest pass rate shows how much is actually remembered.

## Rule 7: the proof marker is checked
- Before it is trusted, the AI marker is tested on write-ups of known quality: official model solutions (must score high) and copies with one deliberate flaw (must score low).
- Where an official STEP mark scheme exists, the marker is given it and marks against it.
- The human reference is the official STEP examiner reports, which record how real examiners awarded marks and the common mistakes candidates made. For questions with a report, write-ups are built that make the mistakes the examiners describe, and the marker must take off marks the way the examiners did.
- The marker's instructions are adjusted until both checks pass. If a person becomes available later, their marks on about 15 written proofs are compared the same way.

## Rule 8: the readiness bar learns from results
- The first timed question unlocks at a share of an exam's topics mastered (60 percent to start).
- Before each timed paper the app records its predicted score; after marking it records the real one.
- The bar adjusts from those results, and the gap between predicted and real scores is shown.

## Reviews and the rest
- Learned and mastered topics come back at growing intervals.
- Gym mode and mixed review earn REP but never master a topic.
- Cohort standing, ratings and the story count mastered topics.

5. Readiness bar learns from results (Rule 8). (new, draft 3)
